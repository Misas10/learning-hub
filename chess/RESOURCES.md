# Chess Resources

## Knowledge

- [Dan Heisman — "When You're Way Ahead" (chess.com blog, updated Jan 2022)](https://www.chess.com/blog/danheisman/when-youre-way-ahead)
  Six principles for winning a won game, from the NM who wrote the _Novice Nook_ column. Use for: anything about converting a material advantage. Primary source for Lesson 1.
- [Dan Heisman — Middlegame Principles](https://www.danheisman.com/middlegame-principles.html)
  Terse list of one-line principles ("Talk to your pieces", "Checks, Captures, and Threats"). Use for: middlegame planning lessons, and as a source of quotable rules. Sibling pages: [Endgame Principles](https://www.danheisman.com/endgame-principles.html), [General Principles](https://www.danheisman.com/generalother-principles.html), [Articles by Subject](https://www.danheisman.com/articles-by-subject.html).
- [NM RobRam — "How To Convert An Advantage" (chess.com, Jan 2026)](https://www.chess.com/article/view/how-to-convert-chess-advantage)
  Practical conversion checklist built around two questions: "How can I ruin this?" and "What's the easiest way to convert?". Use for: conversion habits.
- [Chess.com Terms — Piece Exchanges](https://www.chess.com/terms/exchanging-pieces)
  Short definitional page on when to exchange. Use for: glossary-level claims about trades.
- [Exeter Chess Club — General Middlegame Advice](https://exeterchessclub.org.uk/content/general-middlegame-advice)
  Long-running club coaching handouts (Dr. Dave Regis). Use for: "what do I do when there's nothing to do" — improving the worst piece, judging exchanges.
- [Lichess Practice](https://lichess.org/practice)
  Free interactive drills: piece checkmates, [key squares](https://lichess.org/practice/pawn-endgames/key-squares/xebrDvFe/S8rbDQmn), [opposition](https://lichess.org/practice/pawn-endgames/opposition/A4ujYOer). Use for: endgame skill practice with instant feedback.
- Stockfish (local, in the teacher's scratchpad)
  Every quiz position in a lesson must be engine-checked before it ships. Use for: verifying claims about concrete positions, never for explaining *why*.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/chessbeginners](https://www.reddit.com/r/chessbeginners/)
  Large beginner-focused subreddit. Use for: posting a game and asking "where did this go wrong?". _Not yet vetted by the learner — moderation quality unconfirmed._
- Chess.com post-game review + the opponent's chat
  Use for: fast feedback after every rapid game.
